Description	It has long been observed that words that correspond to a potential sensory experience—concrete words—have a processing advantage over abstract words in various lexical tasks. We collected concreteness norms for a set of 1,659 French words, as well as other psycholinguistic norms that were not available for these words—contextual availability, emotional valence, and arousal—but which are important if we are to gain a better understanding of the significance of concreteness effects.
Auteurs	Bonin, P., Méot, A., & Bugaiska, A.
